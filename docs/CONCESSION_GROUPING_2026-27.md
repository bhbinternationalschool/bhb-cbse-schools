# Who gets the same discount — and the four that need a decision this week

Run against production on 2026-09-08, scoped to the running session (2026-27).
Only **live** grants are counted: approved, not superseded, under an active
rule. An earlier pass that counted every approved row overstated the groups,
because changing a child's discount at the counter *ends* the old grant and
opens a new one, so a child who moved from ₹190 to ₹1,900 appeared twice.

### APRAJITA MISHRA looks like a typing slip, not a decision

Her tuition discount was **₹190 until 9 August, then ₹1,900 from 10 August** —
exactly ten times, against a tuition of exactly ₹1,900.

The effect is already visible in the books: her tuition rows for **August and
September have disappeared from open dues entirely**, because ₹1,900 billed
minus ₹1,900 discount is nothing left to owe. She still shows an Examination
Fee due for September, so she is very much an active, billed student — her
tuition simply stopped existing. At ₹1,900 a month for the eight months from
August to March that is **₹15,200**, and because the balance is zero she will
never appear on a defaulter list to prompt the question.

Two entries either side of it look like the same slip in the other direction:
JIGYASHA DIXIT ran on **₹24** until 9 August (₹24 against a ₹1,500 tuition)
before moving to ₹262, and the ₹1,900/₹190 and ₹262/₹24 pairs are both a
factor-of-ten apart. Worth checking who entered them.

**This needs a decision, not a fix from me** — if the director granted full
tuition to these families, the entry is right and only the *ground* is
missing. Nothing has been changed.

## 2. The same discount under many names

Twenty distinct amounts are in use. The problem is not the amounts, it is that
each one exists many times over: **36 children get ₹150 off tuition under 29

then one child each on ₹1,900 · ₹1,000 · ₹350 · ₹275 · ₹262 · 10%.

The 100% row is the only one that reads like a policy, because RTE is a policy.
Everything else is the counter creating a fresh definition each time somebody
types an amount, so "₹150 off tuition" is not one rule with 36 children on it —
it is 29 rules that happen to agree.

**Consolidating these is safe and mechanical**: same amount, same head, same
effect. Doing it turns twenty amounts into a price list the office can apply
consistently and explain to a parent who asks why their neighbour pays less.

## 4. What I would do, in order

1. **Decide the four in §1 this week.** Confirm or correct APRAJITA's ₹1,900
   first — it is the one that is silently free and started three weeks ago.
2. **Record the ground on those four**, then work outward. The field now
   exists on every grant, old ones included ([PR #107](https://github.com/bhbinternationalschool/bhb-cbse-schools/pull/107)); the lists show
   "Ground not recorded" in orange until it is filled in.
3. **Consolidate the duplicate definitions** — ₹150 and ₹200 alone are 49
   children under 49 definitions. Safe, mechanical, and it is what turns this
   into a price list.
4. **Then write the sibling rule.** Twenty-four households are being treated
   differently with nothing to point at. Once §3 is a policy, most of these
   answer themselves.
5. **Resolve ABHI PATEL's two records** before anything else is read off them.
